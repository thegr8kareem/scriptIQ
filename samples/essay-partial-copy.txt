Mobile Money and the Unbanked in Ghana

Mobile money has fundamentally transformed access to financial services in Ghana over the past decade. Before its introduction, a large share of the population, particularly in rural communities, had no access to formal banking services. Traditional banks concentrated their branches in urban centres, leaving farmers and traders in remote areas dependent on informal savings groups.

The launch of mobile money services changed this landscape dramatically. With nothing more than a basic mobile phone, individuals can now save money, transfer funds to relatives, and pay for goods and services.

My own survey of thirty traders at Kejetia market found that twenty-six of them received customer payments through mobile wallets daily, and eleven had taken a micro-loan through a mobile lender within the past year. Several respondents explained that the transaction record on their phones had become their first form of documented financial history, which they later used to open formal bank accounts.

These findings suggest that mobile money is not merely a substitute for banking but a stepping stone toward it. Policy should therefore focus on strengthening the bridge between wallet histories and formal credit scoring.
