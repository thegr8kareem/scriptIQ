How Mobile Money Has Shaped Financial Inclusion in Ghana

Over the last ten years, mobile money has fundamentally transformed access to financial services in Ghana. Prior to its arrival, much of the population, especially people living in rural communities, lacked access to formal banking. The traditional banks placed their branches mainly in the cities, which left farmers and traders in remote villages relying on informal savings groups.

The introduction of mobile money services altered this situation dramatically. Using only a simple mobile phone, a person can now keep savings, send funds to family members, and pay for goods and services. The number of registered mobile money accounts in Ghana is now far greater than the number of conventional bank accounts, which shows how rapidly the technology has spread.

Nevertheless, some problems persist. Scams aimed at mobile money users have grown increasingly sophisticated, and the e-levy on electronic transactions introduced in 2022 caused transaction volumes to fall at first as people went back to using cash. In addition, gaps in digital literacy leave older users exposed to social engineering attacks.

To conclude, although mobile money has greatly improved financial inclusion in Ghana, further progress will depend on better consumer protection, ongoing investment in digital literacy, and regulation that does not discourage electronic payments.
